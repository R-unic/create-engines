# The edit of the full trailer: which shots it uses, in what order, for how long. post.py reads it; TrailerCoverageTest checks
# that every shot of client/ShowcaseRun is here exactly once and nothing else is. The words are in captions.txt.
#
#   <shot> <seconds> [at=<s>] [speed=<x>] [lift=<0..1>] [engine] [quiet] [cold] [end]
#
# seconds   how long the piece runs in the cut. Keep it a multiple of 1.5 (half a bar of the music at 80 beats a minute), so
#           every cut falls on the beat.
# at=       where in the shot the piece starts, in seconds of the shot (default: the middle of the shot)
# speed=    plays the shot faster (the sound too, at its own pitch); a slow shot fits in fewer seconds
# lift=     brightens the shadows of a dim shot (0, the default, leaves it as it is)
# engine    the engine is the point here: the music drops and the game's sound comes up
# quiet     no caption of its own; the caption of the piece before stays up over it (montages)
# cold      before the title: game sound only, no music. Only at the start.
# end       the end piece: the title, the links and the AI notice come in over its footage, with a sting, after its own
#           caption has had its time. Last, and only once. There is no end card after it.
#
# A shot that is not in this run (the Aeronautics shots without Sable) is left out of the cut. A chapter (captions.txt
# `chapter <shot>:`) starts with a flash and a hit, and its name is the small gold line over the captions in it.

# The order follows ShowcaseRunShots only where the light changes (day, then the refinery's late sun, then dusk). Inside the
# day it is cut for what is on screen: the biggest camera moves come first and look-alike pads are kept apart. at= is chosen
# so that what a cue does in a shot is on screen: a gasket blowing, the rods changing. An engine whose exhaust or timing has
# just been changed misfires for a few seconds, so those pieces start late in their shots.
